WTI回落、布伦特却走高——这不是"降温",是市场还在犹豫
WTI eases, Brent climbs — this isn't cooling off, it's the market hedging its bets
今早我们提到WTI周四上涨1.63%至$93.66,油价的上涨其实来自伊朗总统佩泽希齐扬在联合国讲话中拒绝美方压力的言论。到了下午,数据出现了分化:WTI回落1.08%至$93.58,但布伦特原油反而走高0.33%,来到$106.25,比今早的$104.70更贵。这种"两桶油走向不一致"的情况,往往说明市场对霍尔木兹海峡局势的判断还没有定论——外交谈判(美伊在联合国边会的接触)与地缘风险(海峡油轮袭击的记忆)在同时拉扯着价格。
This morning we noted WTI rose 1.63% Thursday to $93.66, driven by Iranian President Pezeshkian's UN remarks rejecting US pressure. By this afternoon the two benchmarks diverged: WTI eased 1.08% to $93.58, while Brent actually climbed 0.33% to $106.25 — pricier than this morning's $104.70. When the two crude benchmarks move in opposite directions like this, it usually signals the market hasn't made up its mind on the Strait of Hormuz — diplomatic contact (the US-Iran sideline talks at the UN) and geopolitical risk (memories of tanker strikes in the strait) are pulling the price in different directions at once.

We've been tracking this thread since September 21: Amazon blocked Meta's AI shopping agent Muse, citing its agent-access rules. Zuckerberg answered back yesterday (September 24) — Muse will stay free for most users (Meta plans to monetize via a small transaction fee instead) and rolled out a heavyweight roster of retail partners: Walmart, Best Buy, Sephora, Gap, Wayfair, Dick's Sporting Goods, Ulta Beauty, and Fanatics, plus Expedia for travel and Instacart for groceries. JPMorgan's take was blunt: Muse could become "the most widely used consumer AI app since ChatGPT." Meta's stock closed up 1.02% at $744.10 that day.
