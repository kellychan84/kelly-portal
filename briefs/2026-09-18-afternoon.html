WTI dips below $100 as Saudi pipeline repair eases supply fears

Brent fell to $102.74 (-1.99%) and WTI to $100.02 (-1.85%) today, the third straight session of declines. Trading Economics reports Saudi Aramco is rerouting crude via "shuttle vessels" that load tankers outside the Strait of Hormuz, while the war-damaged East-West pipeline is expected to recover roughly half its capacity within days and reach full operation within six weeks. Why it matters: oil has pulled back meaningfully from this morning's ~$101 level — a rare, real risk-premium compression these past two weeks. Kelly's Takeaways

The thing worth noticing this afternoon isn't any single headline — it's that two narratives are running in parallel: "cooling down" (oil sliding further, Saudi pipeline repairs) and "friction continues" (a diplomatic expulsion, a tanker interdiction in Hormuz). The market is currently leaning toward the optimistic read — oil prices reflect it — but Trump's "war is toward the end" comment this morning doesn't fully square with his "anything could happen" tone this afternoon. My advice: welcome the signs of cooling, but don't treat them as certain enough to reposition long-term allocations around.
